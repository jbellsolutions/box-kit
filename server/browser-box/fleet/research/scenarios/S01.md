# S01. Onboard a new client identity, end to end

Stress test, group 1. Read against the design as written on 2026-10-10. **Nothing described here is built.**
Every step below is a walk through the target, not a run.

**Verdict: handled with gaps.** The main path exists on paper: create, human login through the viewer, sleep
with a state bundle, wake the next day with a probe. It fails in the margins: a new identity has no legal
state to log in from (S01-1), there is no creation surface (S01-2), the operator login for the viewer is
undecided (S01-3), the human lease can expire mid-login (S01-4), and the next-day wake applies the cookie
journal last, after a logged-out page load (S01-5). Gaps are in the table at the end.

## What the walk assumes

- Client "Acme" is a new tenant. The owner is Justin or whoever he names. The human who logs in is Acme's own
  authorised person with their second factor in hand (ID §2.2, option 2).
- The platform is Facebook (Meta). A browser identity is only justified for a task with no API, with the
  client's written risk acceptance (ID §2.1 step 0, CH invariant 6). Why not X or LinkedIn: no agent browser on
  x.com and none on LinkedIn at all (CH red lines). See S01-10 for how thin the allowed set is.
- Defaults hold: Q-01 API-first, Q-06 every cap is $0 until Justin names one, Q-08 non-root, Q-13 one client
  per node, Q-12 a droplet exists.
- These do not exist yet and S01 cannot run without them: M1 gates G1.1 (login survives sleep and wake),
  G1.2 (IP and persona stable), G1.5 (the viewer handles a real login); M2 (fleetd, node agent, viewer
  broker); an answer to AQ-1 (how operators authenticate).

## What the design gets right here

- The agent never types a credential and never sees a cookie (CH invariant 4; ARCH §4.3 "credentials are never
  typed").
- A human takeover bumps the fencing token, so the agent's in-flight commands go stale (ARCH §3.5).
- "Ready" means a probe passed, not that Steel answered 200 (CH invariant 1; ARCH §3.3 `auth_markers`).
- A failed upload holds the identity and does not sleep it (ARCH §4.9 step 7).
- The pinned IP and persona are checked on every wake and the forwarder fails closed (ARCH §2.3, §4.1 W9, W11).

## Gaps

Severity: blocker = the scenario cannot finish safely as designed. major = it can finish but unsafely, or only
by guessing. minor = wording, ownership or a missing detail.

| ID | Severity | Target doc | Problem | Proposed fix |

| S01-1 | major | architecture | **A new identity has no legal state to log in from.** ARCH §5.5 says `quarantined` covers "a not-yet-canaried identity". ARCH §3.8 and ID §2.6 say quarantined means no work and no wake. W1 (ARCH §4.1) answers `quarantined` to a wake. The state enum (ARCH §3.2) has no creation or canary state. If a new identity starts `asleep`, nothing enforces the canary. If it starts `quarantined`, it cannot wake for its own login. | Add a `canary` state. Allowed: wake with `purpose=reauth`, the login probe, read-only procedures. Refused: every effect. Take "not-yet-canaried" out of the `quarantined` row in §5.5. | S01-2 | major | architecture | **No identity-creation surface.** DEC D17 says creation is owner-only and not a tool. The provisioner (ARCH §2.7 H4) mints tokens and edits profile config. | S01-4 | major | architecture | **The human lease can expire mid-login.** `takeover` sets the lease to `now() + viewer_ttl` (ARCH §3.5). A slow 2FA (an SMS delay, an "approve on another device" prompt, a checkpoint) can outlive the TTL. Then `acquire` can hand the identity to a pre-wake, or the idle reaper (N1) can sleep the browser mid-login. | S01-5 | major | architecture | **The wake uses the cookie journal last, although the design calls it the source of truth.** ARCH §3.3 and §4.9 say the context export is the source of truth for cookies and the tarball covers the rest. ## Where DECISIONS looks wrong or silent

- **D14** names a "time-limited, authenticated viewer link" but says nothing about who authenticates. ARCH §9.1
  item 3 already says so. S01 shows the client's own staff are one of the people who must authenticate.
- **D2** lists personal profiles in the narrow browser set. SC §6 reads that as "not offered". Reconcile in
  DECISIONS first (S01-10).
- **D4** gives 31 s for the cookie hold. SP and ENG-S default to 35 s. DECISIONS should say which one stands
  after M1 measures it (S01-6).
