# S09. Scale to 1,000 identities across many clients

Stress test, group 3. Read against the design as written on 2026-10-10. **Nothing described here is built.**
Every step below is a walk through the target, not a run. Every figure in this file is arithmetic on a cited
number or a stated assumption, and is labelled **estimate**. None is a measurement.

**Verdict: handled with gaps.** The shape scales: the work is API-first, so most identities never open a browser;
scheduled manifest items run without a Hermes call; every table row, key and bundle is per tenant. The gaps are in
what is shared. One developer app per platform is shared by every client and the governor has no per-tenant share
(S09-4). One decider token budget is shared and a rate-limited call climbs the ladder instead of waiting (S09-6).
The fleetd job claim, the node-creation queue and the operator queue have no fairness rule (S09-3, S09-7). The
premise of one client per node may buy nothing, or may break continuity, and no gate decides which (S09-1, S09-2).
Isolation on shared Hermes hosts is untested (S09-11). Cost has a second axis, the share of identities that need a
browser, that nobody has estimated (S09-9).

## What the walk assumes

These are **illustrative assumptions** chosen to exercise the design. The research has none of them (CON C-17:
"the share of identities that need a browser at all... no one has estimated").

- 1,000 identities across about 200 clients (an average of 5). Defaults hold: Q-01 API-first, Q-13 one client per
  node, Q-06 caps named by Justin, Q-07 both ZDR routing and consent.
- *f* = the share of identities that need a browser. Worked example: *f* = 15%, so B = 150 browser identities
  (login and re-auth, `research` identities for open-web lead work, the no-API set). The other 850 are API-only.
- 600 identities have a manifest item at the same local morning window. Most clients sit in one or two time zones.
  Browser identities are spread over C = 100 clients.
- Nothing is built. M1 to M6 gates are assumed passed so that the design can be stressed at its largest size.

## The numbers

All figures are **estimates**. Sources are named per row. Unit prices are `vendor-claim` or `official-docs` as cited.

### A. Decider demand against provider limits

Method (S1M §2.5): A = browser identities in an L2-heavy phase at the same time, one decision every p = 3 s (the
research assumes 2 to 3 s; 3 s is the favourable end). Tokens per 180-candidate step: 4,911 to 6,217 (measured,
S1V V-04). Jev's limit: 100K tokens/s, 80 rps, "dynamic" (`official-docs`, S1V S1-03).

| A | Requests/s | Tokens/s at 4,911 | Tokens/s at 6,217 | Share of Jev's 100K tokens/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 | 10 | 49,110 | 62,170 | 0.49 to 0.62 |
| 60 | 20 | 98,220 | 124,340 | 0.98 to 1.24 |
| 150 | 50 | 245,550 | 310,850 | 2.46 to 3.11 |

- With 60-candidate steps (about 2,200 to 2,500 tokens; S1M §2.5 estimate), A = 150 is 110,000 to 125,000 tokens/s,
  1.10 to 1.25 times Jev's limit. Accuracy at 60 candidates is not measured.
- Best case with perfect spillover: Jev 16.1 to 20.4 steps/s, Decider at most 10 (10 rps per org), Clef at most 5
  (an assumption, S1V S1-03), OpenAI Build 5.4 to 6.8 (only if the tier applies to Decisions, unconfirmed). Total
  36.5 to 42.2 steps/s. At A = 150 and 50 requests/s the shortfall is about 8 to 14 steps/s.
- That spillover works only for a template with a calibrated row on each provider (S1M §3.7). Decider and OpenAI
  serve screenshot questions, so they are not interchangeable with Jev's text-state questions.
- **API-first changes the picture.** The 600 morning posts are L0 and never reach a decider. A is the number of
  browser identities in an L2 phase, not 1,000 (S1M §2.5: "real demand is lower where API-first work keeps
  identities out of the browser").

### B. Node creation and the one-client-per-node rule

| | Q-13 yes (one client per node) | Q-13 no (packed) |
|---|---|---|
| Nodes for B = 150 awake, C = 100 clients | at least 100 (each client's browser identities fit one node) | ceil(150 / 7) = 22 |
| Creation batches at 10 at once | 10 | 3 |
| Hourly node cost, estimate | 100 x `s-2vcpu-4gb` at $24 / 672 h = $0.0357/h, so $3.57/h | 22 x `s-8vcpu-16gb` at $0.14286/h, so $3.14/h |
| Lead before the last first-wake | batches x `t_create` + wake p95 | the same, smaller |

`t_create` has never been measured (MS G5.7 measures it in the scale tier). If it were the 60 s billing minimum,
used here only as a placeholder, the lead would be about 10 minutes against about 3. **The dollars are close. The
creation waves, the cold bundle cache on every fresh node, and the number of hosts to run are not.** ARCH §7.1
puts the honest count at `sum over clients of ceil(P_c / slots)`. The 2x peak factor and the 16 h window behind
`P = B x h / 16 x 2` are unsourced (SCV SCA-7), and a clustered morning breaks both.

### D. Cost sensitivity (second axis)

Base row from DEC D12 and MS §7.4: 1,000 identities at 3 browser hours a day, all-browser upper bound: nodes
$2,720 (autoscaled), proxies $2,500, models $2,900, control $283. These sum to $8,403 (D12 states ~$8,413).
Scaling nodes, proxies and models linearly with *f* is my assumption. The models line includes a planner term that
does not scale with *f* ($1,566 of $2,900, SCV SCA-11), so models below are a ceiling.

| Case | Nodes | Proxies | Models (ceiling) | Control | Monthly total, estimate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| *f* = 100% (D12 upper bound) | 2,720 | 2,500 | 2,900 | 283 | 8,403 |
| *f* = 30%, IPs only for browser identities | 816 | 750 | 870 | 283 | 2,719 |
| *f* = 10%, IPs only for browser identities | 272 | 250 | 290 | 283 | 1,095 |
| *f* = 10%, `proxy_id` mandatory for all 1,000 | 272 | 2,500 | 290 | 283 | 3,345 |

X fees come on top, per 1,000 X posts a day (30 days): plain posts 1,000 x $0.015 x 30 = $450, plus read-backs
1,000 x $0.005 x 30 = $150, so $600; link posts 1,000 x $0.20 x 30 = $6,000, plus $150, so $6,150. Outbound DMs at
1,000 a day: 1,000 x $0.015 x 30 = $450. The workloads are invented; the prices are not (SC §4.3 makes the same
warning). The D12 table has no X line at all (CON C-17). It also has no operator time, which is the likely largest
unpriced line (S09-17), and no one-client-per-node line.

## Noisy neighbours and isolation

| Shared thing | Who contends | Control in the design | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| One developer app per platform (splitting is a red line, SC §2.1) | Every client | Governor per-app window (C11) | No per-tenant share; app-level enforcement stops all clients (S09-4) |
| Decider token budget | All browser identities | Limiter in tokens/s; fair queue per identity and client (S1M §3.7) | Single process in v0; weights undefined; rate-limit climbs instead of waiting (S09-6) |
| GLM provider endpoints | All L3/L4 work | Bench-gated allowlist; retry on another provider (ARCH §2.5) | Throughput unmeasured; 429s already seen (RSN); ZDR narrows the list |
| Droplet creation, 10 at a time | All pools | None | Needs a fair share (S09-3) |
| fleetd API and job claim | All tokens | Spend caps stop money, not load | No request-rate or in-flight cap per token or tenant; claim is "oldest queued job" (S09-7) |
| Operator and approval queue | All clients | None | No priority or fairness (S09-7) |
| Spend ledger rows | Every paid call | Reserve before, settle after (C13) | Hot fleet-wide row (S09-14) |
| Hermes host Unix user | Profiles on one host | "Sandboxed backend or per-profile Unix users" (ARCH §6.6) | Untested; a sibling's token or vault may be readable (S09-11) |
| Host-real fingerprint | Every identity on identical software | One client per node | May not unlink (S09-1) |
| IP neighbourhood | Tenants served by one vendor | Per-IP vetting | No cross-tenant subnet rule (S09-13) |
| Vendor, model provider, platform app | Everyone | None | Shared fate; see S07-9 |

What does hold: every table row carries `tenant_id`, with Postgres row-level security **[extension]** (ARCH §6.6);
Spaces prefixes, data keys and evidence are per tenant; `/decide` and the shim never batch across tenants; the L1
cache is per tenant. One thing to settle: ARCH §7.4 says pools are "partitioned by client group", which reads as
several clients per pool (S09-12).

## What the design gets right

- **API-first shrinks the problem.** The 600 morning posts need no browser, no node, no wake (ARCH §4.6, §4.11).
- **Scheduled work does not need Hermes.** Manifest items run inside their window, caps and kill switch with no
  model turn, so 1,000 profiles are not a thundering herd at 09:00 (ARCH §4.11).
- **The decider limiter works in tokens, not requests,** and plans for spillover and re-reading limits that moved
  in three weeks (S1M §2.5, §3.7; DEC D7).
- **Creation is batched by design.** ARCH §7.4 says "created in batches of at most 10"; MS G5.7 has a burst gate.
- **Entry rules.** M7 starts each step only when cost, checkpoint rate and human load are inside Justin's
  thresholds, and holding size is a valid outcome (MS §4.9).
- **The synthetic-load rig** (MS M7b) avoids loading any platform with test traffic.

| Rule | Result |
|---|---|
| CH invariant 8 (client isolation) | Met in the data plane. Not demonstrated on shared Hermes hosts (S09-11). Linkability across nodes is unresolved (S09-1). |
| CH invariant 7 (decider is not a safety control; every brain declared, bounded, logged) | Met. Under load the abstain path moves work to a costlier brain (S09-6). |
| CH invariant 9 (pinned proxy only) | Met per identity. Vendor concentration unbounded (S07-9). |
| CH "spend beyond an approved cap" | Reserve-and-settle exists (C13). A webhook-billed X event cannot be refused after the cap (S09-10). |
| CH red line "identical posts across client accounts" | The duplicate-content check is per publish (SC §2.1). At 600 items in one window it is a cross-tenant read on the hot path; its cost is unspecified. |

| "Random jitter prevents flagging" | Unverifiable (ID §6 signal 2, SPV V-13). Window-aware smoothing here is for capacity only, never a detection claim. |
